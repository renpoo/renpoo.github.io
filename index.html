Current work

Tensor-Link Utility

An open analysis platform that models transaction data — double-entry ledgers, urban traffic, stock-market order books, fMRI brain-signal time series — as a mass-spring-damper network, and flags anomalies using quantities borrowed from classical mechanics, thermodynamics, and so forth: entropy (how spread out or concentrated the energy flow is), conservation residuals (whether what flows in matches what flows out), the spectral radius of the transition matrix (a measure of how strongly a cycle feeds back on itself), and an energy-like functional (a single number that rises when the system is under strain). The physical analogy is a modelling choice, not a claim that ledgers obey physical law. Validated so far on synthetic cases; work on real data is ongoing, and I am in discussion with researchers whose prior work overlaps with this.

It also ships a protocol that constrains an LLM to test its own false positives and false negatives before it reports anything.

Technical explainers, in two languages (JA & EN)

Since 2025 I have written for business readers who are not engineers. Each piece starts from something on the reader's desk — a spreadsheet that freezes, a payment that always runs late, a search that comes back empty — and works backward to the structure underneath. The metaphors carry the explanation: streaming as noodles in a bamboo flume, a bill of materials as a family tree, an organisation's strain as the joints of a robot arm. I assume no vocabulary, and full intelligence. The lesson is meant to outlast the tool.

Earlier work

Miranda

A muscle-rigged human maquette for Autodesk Maya, released under CC BY 4.0 in 2013 and downloaded over 250,000 times. Re-engineered from a Maya Muscle system into a skinCluster + Blendshape rig, halving the package from 525.9 MB to 265.3 MB while keeping muscle bulge and relaxation. Both versions are still maintained.

xcor-Analyzer

A MATLAB tool that renders sound as correlation-function terrain. I built it as an unpaid collaborator with Ken Ito’s group at the University of Tokyo, to study concert-hall acoustics. From a stereo recording alone it recovered 68 cm of a singer’s movement across the room. Three peer-reviewed papers came out of this work, all with me as first author.

github.com/renpoo/xcor_Analyzer

Overlay maps of Kawasaki

With the civic group Open Kawasaki, I overlaid elevation, housing-complex locations, shopping streets, rail lines, and ageing-population statistics onto one map, to make the city’s food-desert problem visible where spreadsheets could not.
