Current work

Tensor-Link Utility (2026–)

An open analysis platform that models a double-entry ledger as a mass-spring-damper network and flags anomalies using quantities borrowed from classical mechanics and thermodynamics: entropy (how spread out or concentrated the flow is), conservation residuals (whether what flows in matches what flows out), the spectral radius of the transition matrix (how strongly a cycle feeds back on itself), and an energy-like functional (a single number that rises when the system is under strain). The physical analogy is a modelling choice, not a claim that ledgers obey physical law.

Validated so far only on synthetic cases: a model company's books, and test sets shaped like urban traffic, order books, and brain-signal time series. Work on real data has not started. I have been in contact with one researcher whose prior work overlaps with this.

It also ships a protocol that requires an LLM to test its own false positives and false negatives before it reports anything. The code was written with AI coding agents under test-driven review; the design, the tests, and the limits stated above are mine.

Python · Dockerised · test-driven · documented in Japanese and English · AGPL-3.0 · github.com/renpoo/TLU

Technical explainers, in two languages (2025–)

Written for business readers who are not engineers. Each piece starts from something on the reader's desk — a spreadsheet that freezes, a payment that always runs late, a search that comes back empty — and works backward to the structure underneath. The metaphors carry the explanation: streaming as noodles in a bamboo flume, a bill of materials as a family tree, an organisation's strain as the joints of a robot arm. I assume no vocabulary, and full intelligence.

Earlier work

Miranda (2004–2019)

A muscle-rigged human maquette for Autodesk Maya, developed from 2004 and released under a Creative Commons licence in 2013 (now CC BY 4.0). Re-engineered from a Maya Muscle system into a skinCluster + Blendshape rig, halving the package from 525.9 MB to 265.3 MB while keeping muscle bulge and relaxation. Both versions remain available for download; development stopped in 2019.

The trial set on Highend3D has been downloaded 42,406 times and rated 4.6 of 5 (as of October 2026). My own server logs add an estimated 200,000 further downloads of the full package; that figure is mine, not independently checkable.

xcor-Analyzer (2014–2016)

A MATLAB tool that renders sound as correlation-function terrain. I built it as an unpaid collaborator with Ken Ito’s group at the University of Tokyo (August 2014 to August 2016), to study concert-hall acoustics. In one test it recovered 68 cm of a singer’s movement across the room from a stereo recording alone.

Two conference papers came out of this work, both presented by me as first author at the 7th International Symposium on Temporal Design in Architecture and Environment (ISTD-07, The University of Tokyo, 24–25 November 2015), with Ken Ito, Akinori Takei, and Toru Toyoda:

  • “Sound Structure of Ancient Greek Harmonia Modes: Heterodyne Resonance and Differential Bases”
  • “Characteristic Effect for Proximal Narrow-band Digital Stochastic Noises” (“structural timbre”)

Both are listed, with me as presenter, in the public KAKEN record of the group’s grant: KAKENHI 26300019, FY2015 report. Author’s English versions (2018): paper 1 · paper 2 · github.com/renpoo/xcor_Analyzer

Overlay maps of Kawasaki (2015)

With the civic group Open Kawasaki, I overlaid elevation, housing-complex locations, shopping streets, rail lines, and ageing-population statistics onto one map, to make the city’s food-desert problem visible where spreadsheets could not.
